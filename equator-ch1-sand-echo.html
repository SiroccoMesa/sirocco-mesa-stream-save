1. Sand Echo — What the Desert Remembers

The traveler enters a landscape so open that distance becomes difficult to judge.

Nothing appears to move.

Yet the place is not empty.

Wind moves over the sand, uncovering fragments and covering others. Old footsteps disappear. New ones take their place.

A familiar melody seems to arise from somewhere beyond the dunes.

He recognizes it, but not completely.

It is like remembering a childhood story after many years: the shape remains, but time has altered the details.

The traveler follows the melody.

Sometimes it sounds near.

Sometimes impossibly far away.

He eventually understands that there is no musician waiting over the next ridge.

The landscape itself has become the instrument.

The old melody has crossed so much time and distance that it no longer belongs only to the place where it began.

It has become an echo.

And echoes change according to the space through which they travel.

Meaning: Tradition survives by changing as it travels.
